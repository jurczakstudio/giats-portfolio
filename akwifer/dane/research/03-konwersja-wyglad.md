## 2. Ten conversion rules, ranked
The ranking weighs strength of evidence against how much each rule matters for a well-drilling business, where the job is expensive, done rarely and has an uncertain price.

1. **Show a price range instantly and put the calculator at the core of the page, not a "get a quote" form.** Evidence: rows 10, 16, 19, 32. Few homeowners find prices online (15%) even though most look for them. The cream "job card" calculator is the right idea. Make it the hero, not a section further down. Output a range in PLN, such as "~18–26 tys. zł for ~40 m", with the assumptions listed. Then ask for a phone number to "lock in the price after a site visit".
2. **Keep the form short; split it into steps only when it is long.** Evidence: rows 30, 31. For the estimator, ask 2–3 questions (region/municipality, house or plot, rough depth if known). Ask for contact details last, after the price is shown. Field count matters more than step count.
3. **Make calling one tap away and answer the phone.** Evidence: rows 21, 24–27. Use a sticky bottom bar on mobile with "Zadzwoń" and "SMS/WhatsApp" [the SMS/WhatsApp preference in PL is unverified]. Promise a response time, such as "oddzwaniamy w 15 min, pn–sob 7–19". Invoca's data shows that missed calls are the biggest leak.
4. **Put a star rating and review count above the fold, with fresh dates.** Evidence: rows 1–7. Show the Google rating to one decimal place, the review count (aim for 20 or more), and the 2–3 latest reviews with their dates. Show owner replies, because 80% prefer a business that responds. Recency is now a filter, not a bonus.
5. **Show proof of real work: photos, a map of completed wells, technician faces.** Evidence: rows 14, 15. Use a choropleth or point map of drilled wells, labelled "312 studni w powiecie X". Add a crew card with name and photo. A real-photo gallery beats stock images.
6. **Speed is a hard requirement.** Evidence: rows 28, 29. Target LCP under 2.0 s on 4G. The hero must not depend on a heavy WebGL or video payload. Load visual effects after the first paint.
7. **Make the offer concrete and remove risk.** Evidence: rows 34, 35, and Unbounce's advice on free estimates. Offer "Bezpłatna wizyta + wycena na miejscu" and a specific guarantee, such as "no water = you pay only for drilling X m", "fixed price per metre" or a "5-year warranty on the pump". **Test it:** row 35 shows a guarantee can backfire when trust is already high.
8. **Show financing as a monthly payment next to the estimate.** Evidence: rows 13, 36, 37. Example: "od ~490 zł/mies." The causal evidence is weak, but 55% of homeowners expect a payment option. Show it, but keep it secondary.
9. **Build service-area pages, one per county or municipality.** Evidence: row 8 and the AI/local discovery shift; the Think with Google "near me" data is old [S]. Each page needs typical depth, typical price, local geology and nearby jobs. These pages serve both SEO and LLM answers. Keep copy around 300 words above the fold (row 22).
10. **Use urgency only when it is real; skip chat and hero video unless you can test them.** Evidence: rows 23, 38. Show truthful availability, such as "najbliższy wolny termin wiercenia: 14 X", in the calculator result. I found no data supporting a chat widget. Use video for proof (a timelapse of a drill job), not as an autoplay hero.

### Pattern across 2025–26 SOTD industrial/energy winners (observed)
- **Exactly two colours** on almost every Awwwards palette card: one near-black or cream base plus **one loud accent** (orange, coral, hi-vis yellow, lime), or an unusual pairing (blue + cream, green + cream). Nobody uses a muted multi-tone "dusk" palette.
- The stack is mostly **GSAP + Astro/Webflow/Nuxt**. Static-site tooling is normal, even at award level.
- Recurring features: scroll-pinned storytelling, 3D cutaways or schematics, video heroes, mask/wipe transitions, preloaders with counters, oversized all-caps headlines, big numeric deltas.
- Dev scores for **accessibility** are the lowest everywhere (6.6–7.6). There is room to beat them with `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 4. Eight visual moves for a static HTML/CSS/JS site (GSAP or vanilla)

1. **"Zejdź pod ziemię" scroll-scrubbed cross-section (hero → section 2).** Use one tall SVG of the soil profile: humus, clay, sand, gravel, aquifer. Pin it with GSAP ScrollTrigger (`pin:true, scrub:true`) while the drill bit and casing line descend. A depth counter ticks "0 m → 42 m" in huge tabular numerals. Layer labels slide in at their depths. When the drill reaches water, the screen floods blue. Fallback: CSS `animation-timeline: view()` or a static image under `prefers-reduced-motion`. (Refs: RapidKert, Exebenus, ZettaJoule.)
2. **Kinetic depth numerals.** Set the headline numbers at `clamp(6rem, 18vw, 16rem)` in a condensed face; Archivo Condensed is fine, so keep it but go much bigger. Use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`. Count them up with ScrollTrigger and add before/after deltas, for example "dmuchawa: 6 m³/h → 2.8 m³/h zużycie". (Ref: SSTR.)
3. **A two-colour rebrand: drop the dusk palette.** Choose either *hi-vis*: #FFE600 or orange #FF5B2A on #111214, with cream #F4EFE6 for "job card" surfaces; or *water*: deep blue #0057A5 on cream #FFF5E8 with a single orange accent. Put the tokens on `:root`. Use flat colour fields and no gradients.
4. **The calculator as hero "instrument panel".** A live SVG gauge or well-depth ruler updates as sliders move. The price range morphs with a FLIP or number tween. A financing line reads "od X zł/mies.". The CTA "Zarezerwuj termin — najbliższy: 14.10" shows real availability. The calculator is both the conversion engine and the "wow". (Refs: Caeli Energie "project simulation", Houston Roofing.)
5. **Mask-wipe reveals on real job photos.** Animate `clip-path: inset(100% 0 0 0)` → `inset(0)` with ScrollTrigger, with a slight scale-down of the image (1.15 → 1). Use it for a gallery of drilled wells shown as "polaroid job cards" with location, depth, yield and date. (Refs: Fluid Glass, Haven.)
6. **The interactive map as a heat field.** Replace the static choropleth with dots that appear chronologically on scroll ("2009 → 2026") and a running total counter. Clicking or hovering a county shows its typical depth and price range, which also links the service-area pages. Use plain SVG with a GSAP stagger.
7. **Grain and blueprint texture.** Add a subtle SVG `feTurbulence` noise overlay (2–4% opacity), a blueprint grid behind the technical sections, and hand-annotated callouts drawn with `stroke-dashoffset`. This gives a tactile "engineer's notebook" feel at almost no byte cost.
8. **A sticky mobile action bar plus a footer reveal.** On mobile, a bottom bar holds Zadzwoń, SMS and Wycena, and appears after the hero. On desktop, the footer is revealed with `position: sticky` underneath the page and shows a giant phone number (`clamp(3rem, 10vw, 9rem)`) and the reply-time promise. (Refs: Haven footer reveal, Invoca data.)

**Performance guardrails:** Ship GSAP core + ScrollTrigger (~70 KB min, about 25–30 KB gz [approximate, not measured]) with `defer`. Serve the LCP image or SVG inline with `fetchpriority="high"`. Avoid any autoplay hero video over 1 MB. Run every effect behind `matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)')`.
